\begin{FEinfographic}{ch:05}{can simple trend rules beat buy-and-hold after correcting for the number of rules tried?}

\infopanel{1}{Three kinds of trend rule}{%
\infocards
  {Moving averages}{Buy when a short average of the price rises above a long one, and sell when it falls below.}
  {Breakouts}{Buy when the price rises above its recent high, and sell when it falls below its recent low.}
  {Time-series momentum}{Hold each market long or short by the sign of its own past twelve-month return.}}

\infopanel{2}{Where the evidence stands: what published studies found}{%
\begin{infoscore}
\scoreitem{y}{Yes}{Stock-index rules in the original sample}
\scoreitem{n}{No}{Stock-index rules after 1986}
\scoreitem{y}{Yes}{Time-series momentum across many markets}
\scoreitem{y}{Yes}{A century of trend following}
\scoreitem{n}{No}{Asset by asset}
\scoreitem{o}{Unclear}{After 2009}
\scoreitem{y}{Yes}{In long market declines}
\end{infoscore}}

\infopanel{3}{Key numbers}{%
\infonumbers
  {26}{rules tested by Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron}
  {58}{contracts, all profitable for time-series momentum}
  {1880}{start of a century of positive trend returns}
  {2022}{a strong year when stocks and bonds fell together}}

\infopanel{4}{Lab: Trend rules and the cost of searching}{%
\begin{infolab}
\item \textbf{A.} The 26 rules on the daily U.S. market, before and after 1987.
\item \textbf{B.} A few thousand rules tested with White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA test.
\item \textbf{C.} AQR's time-series momentum factors before and after 2009. \textbf{D.} The smile against market returns.
\item \textbf{E.} About a dozen assets tested one by one and pooled.
\end{infolab}}

\infosummary
  {Classic trend rules on a single stock index do not survive a full accounting for the number of rules tried, while diversified trend following across many markets has a stronger record, though its return is smaller and less certain than first reported.}
  {Use diversified, slow trend following as a diversifier for a stock-heavy portfolio, not as a stand-alone bet, and judge its fee against a transparent rule.}
